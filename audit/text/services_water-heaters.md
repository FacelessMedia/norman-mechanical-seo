Determining when your water heater requires professional repair or outright replacement can be challenging without the right knowledge. Recognizing the signs of a failing water heater can help you act promptly, ensuring you maintain a comfortable, safe, and efficient home. Below are six warning signs to watch for. Trust Norman Mechanical to provide expert advice and service when these issues arise. Lack of Hot Water: If your morning showers are becoming increasingly colder, it is a clear sign that your water heater is struggling. This could be due to a malfunctioning heating element or a more significant issue within the unit. Unusual Noises: Banging, rumbling, or knocking sounds coming from your water heater tank indicate sediment buildup. This not only affects efficiency but can also lead to more serious damage if not addressed. Water Leaking Around the Unit: Any signs of water pooling around your water heater may suggest a leak. Leaks can result from various issues, ranging from loose connections to a crack in the tank. Rusty Water: If the hot water from your taps is discolored or has a rusty appearance, it may mean the inside of your tank is corroding. This usually indicates the anode rod needs replacement or it is time for a new unit. Inconsistent Water Temperature: Experiencing fluctuating water temperatures can signal a failing thermostat or a buildup of minerals affecting the heating elements. Old Age of the Unit: Most water heaters have a lifespan of about eight to twelve years. If yours falls within or beyond this range and is experiencing issues, it may be more cost effective to replace it rather than repair. Water Heater Replacement: How To Choose The Right Unit Deciding to replace your water heater is a significant step towards enhancing the comfort and efficiency of your home. However, with a myriad of options available on the market, choosing the right unit that meets your needs can be daunting. Here are five critical factors you should consider, ensuring you make an informed decision: Energy Efficiency: Look for water heaters that have a high Energy Factor (EF) rating. Units with higher EF ratings use less energy, which can significantly reduce your utility bills over time. This is not only good for your wallet but also better for the environment. Fuel Type: Consider what energy source is readily available in your area and which one will be most cost effective in the long run. Options include electricity, natural gas, propane, and solar energy. Each has its pros and cons related to cost, availability, and environmental impact. Capacity: Choose a water heater with a tank size that adequately meets your household’s water usage needs. Think about how many gallons of hot water you use during peak hours. A too small tank will run out of hot water quickly, while a too large tank will unnecessarily increase energy consumption and bills. Recovery Rate: The recovery rate refers to how quickly a water heater can replenish hot water once it has been depleted. If your household uses a substantial amount of hot water, consider a unit with a fast recovery rate to ensure a continuous supply. Warranty and Longevity: Look for units with long warranty periods as they can indicate the manufacturer’s confidence in the product’s durability. A longer warranty might mean a slightly higher initial cost, but it can save you money and hassle over the long term. Water Heater Maintenance: What To Expect During a Professional Maintenance Service Regular maintenance is key to extending the life of your water heater and ensuring it operates efficiently and safely. When you schedule a maintenance service with our professional plumbers, you can expect the following: Inspection of the Anode Rod: The plumber will check the condition of the anode rod, which helps prevent corrosion inside the tank. If it is significantly worn down, they may recommend replacing it to protect the tank’s integrity. Tank Flushing: Sediment buildup can impair your water heater’s efficiency. The plumber will drain the tank to remove sediment, ensuring optimal performance and reducing the risk of overheating. Temperature and Pressure Relief Valve Check: This safety mechanism protects against excessive temperature and pressure. The plumber will test the valve to ensure it is functioning correctly, replacing it if necessary. Thermostat Settings Review: The plumber will check the thermostat to confirm it is set at an optimal temperature for both energy efficiency and your comfort needs. Heating Element Inspection: For electric water heaters, the heating elements will be inspected for signs of wear or damage and replaced if they are not functioning efficiently. Visual Leak Check: The entire unit, including connections and pipes surrounding the water heater, will be inspected for signs of leaks or corrosion, preventing future problems and ensuring the system is secure.
